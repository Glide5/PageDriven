The challenge

Good traffic was slipping away before the message could land.

CaseLeap was already attracting the right visitors: US marketing teams looking for testimonial video production. But the homepage was losing them within seconds. The message, proof and next step weren't landing fast enough to hold attention.

What the data showed

Visitors left within seconds

Homepage visitors averaged 3 seconds before leaving. The message, proof and next step weren't landing fast enough.

Proof sat too low

Trust signals and testimonials were far below the fold, long after visitors had formed their first impression.

Competing calls to action

The primary CTA competed with equally weighted alternatives, splitting attention at the decision point.

Form friction

Forms added avoidable effort at the moment visitors were ready to act.

Mobile conversion friction

The mobile experience added extra friction at the decision point.

The strategy

Let behavior data decide what changes first.

Rather than redesigning on instinct, every decision was grounded in what GA4 and Microsoft Clarity showed about real visitor behavior on the page.

  1. 1

    Use GA4 and Microsoft Clarity to find where the homepage loses attention.

  2. 2

    Refine the message so the value is clear within the first few seconds.

  3. 3

    Surface proof earlier, closer to the headline and decision points.

  4. 4

    Clarify the CTA hierarchy so the primary action stands out.

  5. 5

    Reduce conversion friction in forms and the mobile experience.

Before and after

What changed on the homepage.

Before
  • Homepage visitors averaged 3 seconds before leaving
  • Trust proof and testimonials sat far below the fold
  • The primary CTA competed with equally weighted alternatives
  • Forms and mobile UX added friction at the decision point
After
  • Outcome-led hero messaging built around business results
  • Trust and testimonials moved closer to the decision point
  • Clearer primary and secondary CTA paths
  • Simpler forms and improved mobile conversion UX

Outcome

Engagement moved decisively in the right direction.

In the measured period after Iteration 1, average time on the homepage rose from 3 seconds to 18 seconds, events per homepage visit rose from 3.34 to 4.84, and quick backs fell from 21.4% to 11.8%. The homepage now holds attention long enough for the proof, the message and the next step to do their job.
